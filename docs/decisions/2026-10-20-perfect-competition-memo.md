# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant **10 tomato beds**, **20 carrot beds**, and **30 mesclun beds** to achieve a maximum seasonal net profit of **$42,761.66**, and prioritize expanding the 20-bed carrot limit first if negotiating additional crop capacity.

## Why

* **Tomatoes hit a labor cost wall at Bed 10**: While tomatoes bring in a top-line $8,800.00 per bed, compounding labor penalties make the 11th bed cost $9,390.72 to produce—costing $590.72 more than it earns. Stopping tomato planting at 10 beds protects farm profit.
* **Carrots and mesclun are artificially choked by crop limits, not farm size**: At their respective limits of 20 and 30 beds, carrots and mesclun still generate strong profit above variable costs ($1,688.95 MC vs. $2,094.00 price for carrots; $2,420.10 MC vs. $2,700.00 price for mesclun). Unlocking a 21st carrot bed yields **+$352.49** in profit, while a 31st mesclun bed yields **+$246.47**.
* **General land and labor are idle and worth $0.00 to expand**: The farm leaves 4 land beds unused (60 of 64 planted) and 1,202.78 labor hours unworked. Buying generic farmland or hiring base workers adds zero value; value lies entirely in lifting specific crop caps.

## The judgment call

The model proves that relaxing the carrot cap adds $352.49 for the first additional bed and the mesclun cap adds $246.47, but the cost and practical steps to lift these limits—such as renegotiating buyer contracts, landlord agreements, or crop rotation rules—exist outside the model. Because these marginal gains diminish with further expansion, the judgment call is that the owner should pursue lifting the carrot cap first, provided the administrative or deal-making cost to unlock the first bed remains under $352.49.

## What would change my answer

My recommendation to maintain 10 tomato beds is most sensitive to tomato market prices and would change if the price drops by more than 6.3% (below $8,248.59/bed), at which point Bed 10 becomes unprofitable and production should shrink to 9 beds.
